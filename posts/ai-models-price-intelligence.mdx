Choosing an AI model is rarely just about finding the highest score. A model can be excellent and still be a poor choice for everyday work if it costs too much. A cheaper model can be a great deal, but only if it is capable enough for the task.

This chart compares **100+ models** using two dimensions:

- **AI Intelligence Index** on the vertical axis: higher means stronger overall benchmark performance.
- **Blended price** on the horizontal axis: an approximate cost per 1 million tokens, shown on a logarithmic scale.

## How to read the chart

The dotted lines split the chart at an intelligence score of 50 and a blended price of $1 per million tokens:

- **Cheap and Smart**: the most attractive area for high-volume work.
- **Expensive and Smart**: strong models that may be worth the extra cost for difficult tasks.
- **Cheap and Weak**: inexpensive models that can still be useful for simple work.
- **Expensive and Weak**: models where the price is harder to justify from this comparison alone.

The horizontal axis is logarithmic. This keeps a model costing a few cents visible next to models costing several dollars or more. It also means that equal visual distances represent ratios rather than equal dollar differences.

## A note about the data

This is a snapshot rather than a permanent ranking. Model versions, benchmark scores, provider pricing, and routing options change frequently. Prices are blended estimates, so the real cost for a particular workload will depend on the input/output mix and the provider you use.

The underlying comparison is based on the [Artificial Analysis model index](https://artificialanalysis.ai/models). Use it as a starting point for exploration, then check the latest model card and pricing before making a production decision.
